# AD-3 / eSEN CPU pilot (2026-09-26)

These are integration diagnostics, not a claim of useful MD acceleration. The host was an M2 Pro Mac without an available PyTorch GPU. The teacher was OpenMM AMBER14/OBC1 velocity Verlet at 0.5 fs; the student was eSEN-sm-direct-all-omol with cloned force heads. We used the first 2,048 frames of the AD-3 train trajectory for adaptation and eight evenly spaced initial states from the first 2,048 frames of the separate AD-3 test trajectory for rollouts.

Force adaptation used 1,000 updates, batch size 4, and learning rate 2e-5. PDD used 1,200 updates, batch size 4, block sizes 1/2/4, and at most two on-policy prefix blocks. The direct L4 baseline used 1,200 updates, batch size 4, and the same initial adapted force checkpoint and learning rate. Runs used one seed (7).

## Held-out force accuracy

On 32 evenly spaced test states, force RMSE against the recreated OpenMM teacher was 0.525 eV/Å for the original eSEN checkpoint and 0.042 eV/Å after adaptation. The stored AD-3 force had 0.419 eV/Å RMSE against this teacher on the same states. These are distinct force definitions, so the adaptation used the recreated teacher labels.

## Trajectory rollouts

Position endpoint RMSE is in Å. `finite` counts states with finite coordinates, velocities, and final energy. A finite result may still be physically unusable. All trajectory methods were compared with the same fine teacher at matching block endpoints.

| Method | Backbone calls | 10 fs position RMSE | 10 fs finite | 40 fs position RMSE | 40 fs finite |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PDD L1 | 20 / 80 | 0.00913 | 8/8 | 0.0612 | 8/8 |
| PDD L2 | 10 / 40 | 0.0155 | 8/8 | 0.101 | 8/8 |
| PDD L4 | 5 / 20 | 0.0246 | 8/8 | unusably large on the sole finite path | 1/8 |
| Direct L4 | 5 / 20 | 0.0249 | 8/8 | — | 0/8 |
| Coarse OpenMM Verlet L4 | 10 / 40 force calls | 0.00409 | 8/8 | 0.0123 | 8/8 |

At 10 fs, PDD L4 and direct L4 had similar error. At 40 fs, both learned L4 methods failed on most or all tested initial states. The exact-force coarse integrator remained finite and much more accurate. The PDD L4 CPU rollout was slower than fine-step classical OpenMM here; this teacher is much cheaper than the eSEN student. A GPU experiment with an expensive MLIP energy-gradient teacher is required to test the intended acceleration claim.

The result identifies the next research target: improve multi-step head accuracy and rollout stability before making speed claims. Force matching alone did not solve the long-rollout error. Full machine-readable per-state metrics are produced by `pdd-md evaluate` and were kept outside Git with the local checkpoints.
